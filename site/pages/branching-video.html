Branching video, built from clips you already have

A branching video lets each viewer choose where to go next. Most tools for it want you to upload footage, learn an editor and pay per viewer. Rabbit Hole builds branches from YouTube videos instead, in your browser, for free.

Two kinds of branch

Branching video usually means a choose-your-own-adventure story: pick A or B and the plot splits. Rabbit Hole is built for a different kind of branching, the kind learning needs:

  • The main track is the shortest path through the topic. Anyone can watch it top to bottom and get the whole story.
  • Deeper tracks hang off individual clips. They hold what some viewers need and others don't: background, a worked example, the maths, a counter-argument. Viewers swipe right to go in and left to come back to exactly where they were.

Because deeper tracks can have deeper tracks of their own, a path can go several levels down, which is where the name comes from.

What you can build

  • A course module: one lecture-length main track with optional refreshers and extensions.
  • An explainer with footnotes: the short version on the main track, the long version one swipe away.
  • A pitch: the two-minute story on the main track, with “how does that work?” and “why now?” clips under the claims that invite questions.
  • Onboarding: the essentials for everyone, with role-specific detail underneath.

Questions

Do I need to edit or upload video?

No. You paste YouTube links and choose start and end times. Videos play in YouTube's own player.

Can viewers choose between options, like a story game?

Partly: any clip can lead deeper, and viewers decide whether to go in. There are no multiple-choice prompts or scoring.

Where is my branching video stored?

In the link. The whole structure is encoded into the URL you share, so there's no account and nothing on a server.
