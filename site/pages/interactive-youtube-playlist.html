Rabbit Hole › Interactive YouTube playlists

An interactive YouTube playlist, with side paths

A playlist plays in one fixed order. A rabbit hole keeps that order as its main track and adds optional videos under any clip, so viewers can dig into one point and come straight back.

Build one now

From playlist to rabbit hole in five minutes

  1. Open the builder and give the path a title.
  2. Paste the playlist's videos one by one, in order. That's your main track.
  3. Find the points where viewers get stuck or curious. Under those clips press + Deeper and add the videos that answer the question.
  4. Trim long videos with start and end times, so a clip is only the part that matters.
  5. Copy the link, or press Embed to put the player on your own page.

What YouTube allows, and what it doesn't

Rabbit Hole only uses YouTube's official embedded player, in privacy-enhanced mode. That keeps it within YouTube's rules for embedding:

  • Videos play from YouTube, with YouTube's controls and branding, and count as views for the creator. Nothing is downloaded, copied or re-hosted.
  • Only videos whose owners allow embedding can be used. The builder checks each link; if an owner later turns embedding off, the player says so and offers the video on YouTube.
  • Start and end times use YouTube's own player settings, not edited copies.
  • Nothing covers the player. Navigation happens below it.

Tips

  • Keep the main track under ten clips; put the rest deeper.
  • Write a one-line note on each clip saying why it's there. The note shows under the video.
  • Mix sources freely: your own uploads and the best explanation from another channel can sit side by side, each credited by YouTube's player.

Questions

Can I import a whole YouTube playlist at once?

Not yet: paste videos one at a time. Importing playlists would need YouTube's Data API, which needs a key; it's on the list for later.

Do viewers need to sign in?

No. Anyone with the link can watch, on any device with a modern browser.

Does the creator still get the view?

Yes. Every video plays in YouTube's own player, the same way any embedded video does.
