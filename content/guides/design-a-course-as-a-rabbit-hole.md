Every course has the same tension: beginners need more explanation, experienced learners want less. A linear video course has to choose one audience. A rabbit hole doesn't: the main track serves the confident learner, and deeper tracks catch everyone who needs more.

## The main track is the promise

Write the main track so that someone who already has the prerequisites can watch it top to bottom and finish with the course's promise fulfilled. Every clip on it should be necessary. If you can delete a clip and the next one still makes sense, it belongs deeper.

## Three kinds of deeper track

1. **Prerequisite:** "you'll need this to follow the next clip." Put it under the clip that needs it, not at the start of the course, so people only meet it when it matters.
2. **Proof or worked example:** the same idea in detail. Under the clip that states the idea.
3. **Extension:** where the idea goes next. Under the last clip of a section, or the clip that hints at it.

Label them in the clip notes ("Rusty on vectors?", "The maths, step by step", "Where this leads"), so viewers can tell which kind they're looking at before they go in.

## Keep detours short

A deeper track of two or three clips is easy to come back from. Longer than that, and it's really a second course: give it its own rabbit hole and link to it from a note.

## Test with two viewers

Ask one person who knows the subject and one who doesn't to watch it. The expert should be able to stay on the main track without feeling it drag. The beginner should go deeper at least twice and come back without getting lost. If the beginner gets stuck on the main track, a prerequisite is missing; if the expert keeps going deeper, the main track is too thin.
