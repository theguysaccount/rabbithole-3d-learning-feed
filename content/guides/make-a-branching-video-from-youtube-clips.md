A branching video lets viewers choose their own route. You don't need to shoot or edit anything to make one: the best explanation of almost any topic already exists on YouTube, in pieces. This guide turns those pieces into one path.

## 1. Write the main track first

Before you search for videos, write the shortest version of the story as a list of steps. For "how a neural network learns" that might be: what a network is, how it's scored, how it improves, how the improvement is computed, and where it leads. Five steps is a good size; more than eight and viewers lose the thread.

## 2. Find one clip per step

For each step, find the clearest video that covers it. Prefer short videos, or long ones where you can point at the right minute. Check that the video allows embedding: in Rabbit Hole's builder, a video that doesn't is refused with a message.

Paste each link into the builder in order. That's your main track.

## 3. Add the deeper tracks

Now ask, at each step, "what would make someone stop and look something up?" Those questions become deeper tracks:

- **Background:** the idea this step assumes ("rusty on vectors?").
- **The worked version:** the same idea with the maths written out.
- **What's next:** where this idea leads, for the curious.

Press **+ Deeper** on the clip and paste the videos for that question. A deeper track can have its own deeper tracks, but two levels is usually enough.

## 4. Trim and annotate

Give long videos a start and end time so each clip is only the part that answers its question. Write a one-line note on every clip saying why it's there ("the derivative intuition that gradient descent depends on"). The note shows under the video and helps viewers decide whether to go deeper.

## 5. Watch it the way a viewer will

Use the preview: swipe or press the arrow keys, go deeper and back out, and check that every detour returns somewhere sensible. If a deeper track only makes sense after the next main clip, move it there.

## 6. Share it

Copy the link. The whole path is stored inside it, so there's nothing to publish and no account. Use **Embed** to put the player on a course page or blog.
