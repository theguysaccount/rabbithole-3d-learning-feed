The best explanation of a concept is often three minutes in the middle of a forty-minute lecture. You don't need to edit the video to use just that part: YouTube's player can start and stop where you tell it.

## Finding the right range

1. Open the video on YouTube and find where the explanation starts. Scrub back a few seconds so the speaker's first sentence isn't cut.
2. Note where it ends: the end of the thought, not the end of the sentence that introduces the next topic.
3. In Rabbit Hole's builder, type both times into the clip's Start and End fields.

## Formats that work

The builder accepts `90`, `90s`, `1:30`, `1m30s` and `1:02:03`. Pasting a link that already has a timestamp (`?t=95` or `&t=1m35s`) sets the start time for you.

## Tips

- **Shorter beats complete.** Viewers in a rabbit hole are choosing to be there; a clip that answers one question well beats one that answers three.
- **Mind the context.** If the clip starts mid-argument, say what came before in the clip's note.
- **Leave the end open when the video ends naturally.** An empty End field plays to the end of the video.
- **Credit stays automatic.** The player still shows the channel and links to the full video on YouTube.
